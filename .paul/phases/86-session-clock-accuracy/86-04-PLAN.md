<objective>
## Goal
Repair the instrument that voided 86-03's run, using **only data already on disk**, and re-register
the bars for a confirmatory run. Measure the wheel's ring-down, set the refractory period from that
measurement, add an encoder-side pairing check so a mispair is rejected instead of laundered, and pin
the whole repair with a fixture that reproduces the defect.

**This plan does not produce a B1 measurement and must not be read as one.** It spends zero device
time and zero EAS builds.

## Purpose
86-03 collected 8 sessions and 42 taps and had to throw the answer away: **B3 failed at 83.3%
acceptance against a 90% bar**, because the encoder tap detector over-triggers (10–28 events for ~5
strikes) and **AC-4's rejection rule is structurally blind to the failure mode that actually
occurred** — it compares audio against frames, two readouts of the *video*, so an **encoder** mispair
leaves both in perfect agreement and is marked ACCEPTED carrying a 180–320 ms residual. 10 of the 35
accepted taps sit more than 50 ms from their own session median.

The expensive resource in this phase is **device time and an EAS build**, not compute. Running the
test again on an instrument that launders bad taps would spend that resource to produce another void
run. Repairing the instrument offline first is the cheap ordering, and the 8 sessions already
downloaded are exactly the corpus needed to do it.

<pre_registered_rule>
## The refractory rule, fixed here before the number is known

This section is the plan's reason for existing in this shape. It is written **before** Task 1 runs,
so the constant cannot be chosen to make B1 look tidy.

1. Run `find_taps(..., refractory_s=0.0)` over each of the 8 raw CSVs. This yields **every**
   threshold crossing, including ring-down re-triggers.
2. Take the gaps between consecutive crossings within a session and pool them across all 8.
3. Split the pooled gaps at a classifier `C`: `short` (< C) are candidate ring-down re-triggers,
   `long` (>= C) are candidate true inter-strike intervals. The protocol asks for ~3 s spacing, so
   evaluate at **C = 1.0, 1.5 and 2.0 s**.
4. Let `R = max(short gaps)` and `L = min(long gaps)`.
5. **Valley criterion:** `L > 2R`, and it must hold at **all three** values of `C`.
6. If the criterion holds: `REFRACTORY_S := ceil(1.5 * R / 0.05) * 0.05`, and assert
   `REFRACTORY_S < L / 2`.
7. **If the criterion fails at any `C`, or the assertion fails, NO CONSTANT IS CHOSEN.** The task
   stops and the failure is this plan's result: it would mean ring-down and real strikes are not
   separable by time alone, and a different discriminator (amplitude, sign, decay envelope) is a new
   plan. Do not widen the classifier, do not drop an outlier session, do not pick a round number.

The 1.5x is a safety factor over the largest re-trigger actually observed; the `L/2` assertion keeps
the window clear of the next real strike. Both are stated here so neither can be adjusted later.
</pre_registered_rule>

<measurement_honesty>
## What may and may not be claimed from the void data

**B3 recomputed on these 8 sessions is a legitimate instrument diagnostic. B1 recomputed on them is
not a measurement.** The distinction is not a formality:

- The repaired detector is derived **from this corpus**, so every number computed on it afterwards is
  **in-sample**. The recomputed acceptance rate is therefore an **upper bound** on the repaired
  instrument's health, not an estimate of it.
- The residual is worse than in-sample — it is the quantity the run was voided for. A post-hoc
  per-session median over this run already gives **-1.79 +/- 9.88 ms**, temptingly close to zero.
  **That number may not be quoted, and neither may any successor of it computed here.** It is exactly
  what pre-registration exists to prevent.

The confirmatory run registered in Task 3 is what produces B1. Until it happens, the phase's answer
to "how accurate is the overlay a coach sees" stays **unmeasured**.
</measurement_honesty>

       rather than in a new tuning constant.

    3. **Contention check**: when two audio onsets select the same encoder tap, REJECT both, naming
       the shared tap. A one-to-one pairing is a structural property, not a statistical one.
