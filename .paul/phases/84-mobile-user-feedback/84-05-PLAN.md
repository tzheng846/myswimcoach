## Goal
Ship the last two Phase-84 items in one lane:

- **Item 2 — "video fails to upload sometimes."** Root cause is now **measured, not hypothesised**:
  the app records clips it cannot upload, the server rejects them with a deterministic 413, and
  every trace of that rejection is swallowed. Fix = stop producing over-cap clips, refuse an
  over-cap clip *before* the network, stop burning retries on deterministic failures, and say the
  real reason in the UI.
- **Item 3 — "camera needs better options, such as zoom etc."** Add a discrete zoom ladder to the
  live recording overlay (the only place with a visible frame) and a front/back lens choice to
  Recording Config (the only place it is native-safe).

Item 2 is the phase's only **silent data-loss** bug. **9 of 69** video sessions in the live library
carry the fingerprint of a lost clip — `video_origin_s` written, `video_path` NULL — and the coach
was never told why. Ground-truth footage is what the whole annotation → segmenter-tuning arc
(Phases 47/57/58/59) runs on, so a clip that vanishes costs a training example, not just a
convenience.

Item 3 rides along because **D5** routes `videoQuality` into item 2's lane, which is the same
`CameraView` JSX item 3 must edit. Splitting them would put two plans in one JSX block.

## Grounded facts (measured live, 2026-08-30)

Everything below was produced by a **read-only** probe against the live Supabase project during
planning. Task 1 turns that probe into a committed instrument so these numbers are reproducible
and re-checkable after the fix.

### G1 — H1 is CONFIRMED, and the cap bites at ~36 seconds of video

Encode rate derived from **37 successfully-stored phone clips** (each clip's byte size ÷ its
recovered duration, where `videoDuration = deviceDuration − video_origin_s`):

| | MB/s |
|---|---|
| median | **1.38** |
| observed range | 0.90 – 2.20 |

The server's `MAX_VIDEO_BYTES` is 50 MB (`api.py:1219`). So:

- at the **median** rate the cap is hit at **≈36 s** of video;
- on the **fastest observed device/scene** (2.20 MB/s) at **≈23 s**;
- `recordAsync({ maxDuration: 300 })` (`RecordScreen.js:683`) permits **300 s**, and `autoStopPrefs`
  allows up to **300 s**. The app is free to record ~8× more than it can upload.

### G2 — The ceiling is visible in the stored data

103 objects, 2512 MB total in the `videos` bucket. Phone clips (97 top-level objects):

```
min 1.9   median 25.1   p90 40.9   max 48.3 MB
>=30 MB: 37    >=40 MB: 13    >=45 MB: 5    >=48 MB: 1    >=50 MB: 0
```

A distribution whose maximum stops **1.7 MB short of a hard cap and never crosses it** is the
signature of the cap truncating the tail, not of clips that happen to be small.

### G3 — CONTEXT open question 1 answered **NO**: H2 is refuted; Phase 82 is NOT a prerequisite

A 1-byte probe object uploaded to the `videos` bucket **succeeded (200)** with the bucket at
**2.63 GB against the 1 GB free-tier figure**, and was deleted again. Storage is not rejecting
writes. Phase 82 is still worth doing for cost and hygiene, but it does **not** gate this plan and
this plan must not wait on it.

### G4 — 6 of the 9 lost clips are explained by G1; at most 1 is not

`video_origin_s` set with `video_path` NULL is a direct fingerprint: `VideoOverlayScreen` auto-posts
the origin on mount (`:164-181`) whenever it is null, so the phone reached the overlay with a clip in
hand and the file still never landed. Recovering each lost clip's duration from
`deviceDuration − origin` and pricing it at the G1 median rate:

| created | device dur | recovered clip | predicted size | |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-08-17 19:31 | 39.0 s | **79.7 s** | **110 MB** | over |
| 2026-08-17 19:35 | 29.9 s | 29.0 s | 40 MB | under |
| 2026-08-17 20:11 | 29.9 s | 29.0 s | 40 MB | under |
| 2026-08-17 20:14 | 39.0 s | **39.2 s** | **54 MB** | over |
| 2026-08-17 20:22 | 39.0 s | **49.0 s** | **68 MB** | over |
| 2026-08-17 20:27 | 39.0 s | **39.3 s** | **54 MB** | over |
| 2026-08-17 20:29 | 39.0 s | **39.2 s** | **54 MB** | over |
| 2026-08-17 20:39 | 39.0 s | **39.2 s** | **54 MB** | over |
| 2026-08-20 00:50 | 18.5 s | 17.8 s | 25 MB | under |

The two 40 MB rows sit inside the device-to-device spread (29 s × 2.05 MB/s = 59 MB), so **6–8 of 9
are H1**. The 2026-08-20 row at 25 MB is **not** explained and is the residual (see G7).

### G5 — The 413 is deterministic, and it is invisible three times over

`api.py:1244-1252` rejects on `file.size` (Starlette sets it for a multipart part) falling back to
`Content-Length`; `FileSystem.uploadAsync` MULTIPART sends both. So an over-cap clip 413s **every
time, before any storage work**. Then:

1. `videoUploadQueue.js:95-97` turns it into `Server error (413)` — and `pump` (`:121`) retries it
   **twice more with 3 s + 10 s backoff**, ~13 s spent on an outcome that cannot change.
2. `UploadToast.js:66` renders the constant string `"Video upload failed"`. `job.lastError` is
   stored and never displayed — the one field that names the cause is dropped on the floor.
3. The chip is dismissible and dies with the app, so within a session the only evidence disappears.

The coach's report — *"sometimes it just isn't there"* — is exactly what this stack produces.

### G6 — `x-upsert: true` makes Retry and re-upload safe

`api.py:1279` uploads with `"x-upsert": "true"`, so a repeat upload replaces rather than 409s.
Nothing in this plan needs to reason about partial state.

### G7 — CONTEXT open question 4: the queue's non-persistence stays OUT of scope, with a reason

H3 (in-memory queue dies on restart) is real — `videoUploadQueue.js:7` says so — but G4 attributes
6–8 of the 9 observed losses to H1, and persistence is a genuine chunk of work (there is no
AsyncStorage dependency in the tree; it would mean a new store plus job/file lifecycle rules).
Two facts materially soften it and belong in the record:

- **the footage is not lost.** `saveVideoToLibrary` (`RecordScreen.js:577-589`) copies every
  recording to Photos, so a failed upload costs the *attachment*, not the file;
- **a recovery path already exists** — the web annotate page attaches an external clip to an
  existing session (Phase 67).

→ new STATE item at UNIFY. Do not build it here.

### G8 — H4 is not a bug; H5 is half-addressed; H6 is not implicated

- **H4** (enqueue skipped when the CSV path returns early) is a *consequence*, not a defect: every
  early return in `uploadAndProcess` (`:283-308`) happens because there is no `session_id`, and with
  no session there is nothing to attach a video to. Only a persisted queue (G7) could change it.
- **H5** (13 s retry budget) is addressed only for deterministic failures — this plan stops wasting
  the budget, it does not lengthen it for genuine network failures. Deliberate: the observed
  failures are not slow-network failures.
- **H6** is not implicated by any measurement. ⚠ One fragility found while checking it, worth
  recording but **not** fixing here: `expo-file-system` is **absent from `package.json` and not
  hoisted** — it exists only at `node_modules/expo/node_modules/expo-file-system`, and both
  `RecordScreen.js:7` and `videoUploadQueue.js:8` import `expo-file-system/legacy`. It resolves
  today (65 sessions have stored video, and every CSV upload uses the same module), so this plan
  **reuses the existing import specifier and adds no new one**. → STATE item at UNIFY.

### G9 — expo-camera's iOS internals decide where each control can live

Read from `node_modules/expo-camera/ios/` at the installed `~56.0.8`:

| Prop | Native path | Safe to change **mid-record**? |
|---|---|---|
| `zoom` | `CameraSessionManager.updateZoom()` → `device.lockForConfiguration()` only | **YES** — no session reconfiguration |
| `facing` | `updateDevice()` → session input swap | **NO** |
| `videoQuality` | `CameraView.swift:39-47` → `updateSessionPreset` → `session.beginConfiguration()` | **NO** — must be set at mount |

Consequences that fix the design:

- **zoom belongs in the live recording overlay** — and it is the *only* place it can usefully live,
  because there is **no pre-recording preview**: `onCameraReady` writes START and calls `recordAsync`
  immediately (`:642-683`), so a coach never sees a still frame to frame against.
- **facing and quality belong in Recording Config / at mount.**

### G10 — the zoom scale is exponential and its ceiling is unreadable from JS

`CameraSessionManager.swift:218` is
`device.videoZoomFactor = 1 * pow(device.activeFormat.videoMaxZoomFactor, zoom)`.
`videoMaxZoomFactor` varies by device *and active format* (commonly ~16, but far higher on some), and
JS cannot read it. So `zoom = 0.5` is 4× on one phone and 11× on another.

**Therefore no preset may be labelled with a magnification factor.** Only `zoom = 0` has a truthful
number: exactly 1×.

### G11 — iOS defaults to **1080p**, and `videoBitrate` needs a codec to take effect

- `CameraViewModule.swift:184` — with `videoQuality` unset the view is driven to `.video1080p`. So
  CONTEXT's "at 4K blows straight past 50 MB" is **overstated**; the app has been at 1080p all along,
  and 1080p alone is enough to blow the cap (G1).
- The TS docstring at `Camera.types.d.ts:360` calls the 16:9 values *"Android only"* — **wrong**.
  `CameraRecordingOptions.swift:17-30` maps all of `2160p/1080p/720p/480p` to iOS presets.
- `videoBitrate` is honoured **only inside `if let codec = options.codec`**
  (`CameraVideoRecording.swift:92-99`) *and* only when that codec is in
  `availableVideoCodecTypes` — otherwise the whole branch is skipped, and an unavailable codec
  **rejects `recordAsync`** (`:102`). This is what makes the bitrate route (option-b at the
  checkpoint) two coupled changes rather than one prop.

### G12 — the tree this plan lands on is already dirty, in both repos

`git status` in `swimnetics-mobile` shows 10 modified files: 84-01's four native files, Phase 74's
three (`CLAUDE.md`, `CycleCharts.js`, `BleContext.js`), and **84-02's three — including
`RecordScreen.js` and `RecordingConfigScreen.js`, which this plan also edits.**

84-01 and 84-02 have both **closed their loops** (2026-08-30); 84-02's suite is green at
