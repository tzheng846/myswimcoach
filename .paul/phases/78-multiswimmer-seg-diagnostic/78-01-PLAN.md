<objective>
## Goal
Establish how many DISTINCT swimmers the annotated corpus actually spans, re-score EVERY
segmentation/detection claim against that corpus, and deliver a per-detector verdict
(still-good / degraded / insufficient-n) — **without touching a single production detector.**

## Purpose
Every segmentation number the project quotes (Phase 59 cycle F1s, 75-02 underwater 0.13 s,
76/77 breakout) is stamped "ONE SWIMMER, one pool, one device." STATE.md item 2 flags that
labeled data may already span Tony / Leo / Titus / AlexGroup. Before any further tuning we
need the truth: is the corpus n=1 or n>1, and do the scores hold when we stop pretending it
is one person? This is the confidence audit that gates trusting these detectors on real teams.

## Output
- `tools/annotated_roster.py` — read-only swimmer census of the annotated set.
- Three scorer reports (segmenter / underwater / breakout) captured against live DB.
- `78-01-SUMMARY.md` — the verdict + reconciliation of the "one swimmer" claim.
- PIPELINE.md §8 updated with the resolved count.
</objective>

- `sessions` carries `athlete_id`; `athletes` has `(id, name)`; `sessions.name` is the
  per-session label. STATE flags "AlexGroup" as a STAND-IN athlete whose individual test
  swimmers are encoded in the session NAMES — so distinct `athlete_id` may UNDERCOUNT real
  humans. The census must expand AlexGroup's session names, not just count athlete rows.

  design. Keep them that way; identity lives ONLY in the new roster tool.
